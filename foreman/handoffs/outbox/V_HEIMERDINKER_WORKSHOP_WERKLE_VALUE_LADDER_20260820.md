## Vision

Werkles should be valuable before it asks for money. A free member gets a real
personal product: an Intake, a private Workshop, useful recommendations, people
worth considering, and the ability to create or buy a focused packet. Payment
should expand continuity, collaboration, and included tools—not repair a
deliberately frustrating free experience.

The vocabulary has one learnable transition:

- **Workshop:** one Werkler's private working room.
- **Werkle:** shared work created when two or more Werklers connect their
  Workshops around one business idea, project, or opportunity.

The term must be taught in the sentence where it first appears. It is not a
gatekeeping label, status badge, or synonym for every project.

## Product ladder to test

### Free member

- Create an account and keep a personal Workshop.
- Receive Intake-based recommendations.
- See people who may fit and compare reasons.
- Preview a shared Werkle before committing.
- Buy individual Bellows packets at their posted non-member price.

### Paid member — $9.99/month

- Keep the Personal and free features.
- Bellows packets included rather than bought one by one.
- Ongoing Workshop tools and tailored follow-through.
- When connected to another paid member, open the shared Werkle layer: shared
  plan, decisions, files, tasks, tailored lessons, and relevant outside help.

### Mixed free/paid connection

- Both people can see why the connection surfaced and preview the shape of the
  shared Werkle.
- No fake broken controls or hidden work.
- Paid features are shown as concrete capabilities with a clear reason and
  price; the free product remains useful on its own.

## Product law for this slice

1. Do not manufacture frustration to force conversion.
2. Do not call unfinished features available.
3. Do not make verification, provider costs, or regulated services unlimited
   merely because membership is paid.
4. Do not promise that payment produces a match, outcome, safety, funding, or
   business success.
5. Sell saved time, continuity, included tools, and collaboration—not access to
   basic dignity or an intentionally crippled result.
6. One-time packet prices remain unspecified until a separate pricing review.
