Owner: Heimerdinker / Dink @ BETSY

## Operator outcome

Ben must be able to walk an example of a real Werkle today: two matched people compare their separate Workshops, decide what stays private, what carries forward, what must be rewritten together, what remains disputed, and what is deliberately left behind. The result must HELP a company emerge. It cannot be a decorative preview or shallow advice card.

## Current truth

- Match Deck already ranks synthetic profiles and supports a practice conversation.
- `Preview a Shared Werkle` currently opens a three-card description that does not let the user merge anything.
- The partnership Bellows memo is useful preparation but lives outside a shared formation flow and saves only on one device.
- Persistent multi-member Werkle records and production schema do not exist yet; this slice must not imply that a legal entity, partnership, invitation, or real member record was created.

## Proposed walkable slice

1. **Invitation and consent.** Show the two people and the reason they reached this table. Nothing enters the shared room merely because they matched.
2. **Workshop comparison.** Show source-labelled statements from `Your Workshop` and the selected synthetic member's Workshop side by side.
3. **Decision ledger.** For each useful subject, the walker chooses:
   - `Carry forward` — this statement may enter the shared draft unchanged.
   - `Combine` — both sides contain useful material; Werkles proposes an editable joint statement.
   - `Keep private` — the source statement stays in its original Workshop only.
   - `Park the disagreement` — the issue remains visible and blocks false agreement.
   Nothing disappears. Provenance stays visible.
4. **Shared company floor.** Build a revisitable working draft containing: purpose, first customer/use case, 30-day test, responsibilities, contributions, decision rights, money questions, proof/check needs, and explicit unknowns.
5. **Readiness, not compatibility.** Show counts for agreed, jointly rewritten, private, and unresolved items. Do not emit a compatibility percentage or pretend agreement where none exists.
6. **Exit and revision.** Either person can revise a proposal, preserve their original Workshop, or stop formation without deleting the history. This is preparation, not a legal agreement or company filing.
7. **Value bridge.** Contextual Bellows lessons/tools appear beside real friction: partnership alignment for roles/authority, company starter floor for entity/adviser questions, assumption test for the 30-day test, evidence brief for important claims.

## Walkthrough seed

Use Ben's current owner-bound Workshop when available. Use the currently selected Match Deck practice profile when available; otherwise choose the strongest current synthetic match. ## Natural-language bar

Every sentence must sound like a competent human helping two people make a hard decision. No `proof and sizing`, `carry`, `support band`, psychiatry fog, faux profundity, or advice that only says `clarify the next step`. The interface must explain what changes when a button is pressed and why the decision matters.

## Hard edges

- Local only; synthetic second member.
- No production users, messages, invitations, company creation, signature, legal agreement, provider call, schema, RLS, secret, push, deploy, or spend.
- Never overwrite either source Workshop.
- Never promote a disputed item into the shared draft.
- Do not claim a cousin review until an actual terminal response is harvested.

## Acceptance

- A first-time walker can explain the difference between Workshop and Werkle.
- The user makes at least five meaningful merge decisions and immediately sees the shared company draft change.
- Reload restores the draft for the same owner in the local walkthrough.
- Every shared statement names its source and current agreement state.
- At least one deliberate disagreement makes the experience usefully uncomfortable instead of falsely harmonious.
- Bellows help is attached to the decision that needs it, not dumped into a generic library link.
- Typecheck, focused contracts, and a rendered browser walk pass.
