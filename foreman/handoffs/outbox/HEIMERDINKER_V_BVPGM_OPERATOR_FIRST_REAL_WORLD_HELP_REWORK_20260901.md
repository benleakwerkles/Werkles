## Original product problem

Werkles must help a person or a newly formed Werkle discover concrete real-world resources that move a business forward: suppliers, equipment, professional help, public assistance, banking and credit-union paths, meeting places, commercial space, training, customer channels, permits, and other needs the members may not know to ask about.

The experience must work when participants live in different places. Decatur was only an example. If one person is in Atlanta and another in Columbus, Werkles must distinguish:

- what is useful near each participant;
- what belongs near the chosen operating location;
- what can serve the Werkle statewide or remotely;
- what requires the people to choose a shared meeting area;
- what Werkles does not yet know.

It must not infer or expose a home address, pretend a midpoint is convenient, rank people by wealth, promise eligibility or availability, hide sponsorship, become a directory dump, or repeat the same content across Workshop, Bellows, and the Werkle.

## Product test

A human should be able to answer, quickly:

1. Why did this appear?
2. Who or what location does it serve?
3. What is known, from what source, and as of when?
4. What remains unknown?
5. What can I do next without accidentally sending data or making a commitment?
6. Why is this appearing here—in Workshop, Personal Bellows, or the shared Werkle—instead of as a duplicate page?
